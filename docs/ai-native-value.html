Act 1

The two types of AI software

What AI Enabled and AI Native mean, what each looks like in a working day, and the questions that tell them apart.

1 · The two types

Every AI software product is one of two types: AI Enabled or AI Native.

The difference is not how much AI a product has. It is whether the software was designed for AI as a user, or had AI added to a design made for people.

AI Enabled

AI added to software designed for people

  • People are the primary user. Screens, workflows and interfaces were designed for them.
  • AI reaches the system through the same doors people use: screens, reports and interfaces, often wrapped as tools.
  • It waits to be asked, or it runs one narrow task.
  • What it can learn, act on and touch is limited by the old design.
  • It improves when the vendor ships a release.

Examples: NetSuite, Dynamics 365, Pronto, Aptean, order-capture add-ons, and FoodFlow today.

vs
AI Native

Software designed for AI and people as equal users

  • AI is a first-class user. Actions, data, rules and limits are designed for it first. Screens are another view.
  • It works through governed actions built for it: limited, reversible and backed by evidence.
  • It watches the business and acts first, inside rules the business sets.
  • Learning, identity, limits and audit are part of the core, not added later.
  • It improves every week inside each business, from that business's own corrections.

Examples: FoodFlow on AXIS, the target. A few young AI-first vendors, in narrower areas.

Why legacy cannot cross the line

4 · Why legacy stalls

Established suites stay AI Enabled because their design, economics and customers all hold them there.

NetSuite and other legacy stacks have money, talent and data. They add AI quickly. They cannot easily cross the line, because crossing it means changing the things that made them successful.

The bolt-on pattern. A connector (for example MCP) exposes the doors built for people to an assistant or agent. Nothing underneath changes.

Eight bottlenecks, by dimension

Can an incumbent catch up? It has three options.

Option 1

Deepen the AI Enabled layer

More assistants, more prebuilt agents, more connectors. Fast and safe for the installed base.

Ceiling: every gain still passes through the old design, so the limits above stay.

Option 2

Build an AI Native product beside the suite

A new core, with a migration path for customers. This is the only route across the line.

Cost: a second product, a long overlap, and a threat to the revenue of the first.

Option 3

Buy an AI Native entrant

Acquire the stack and the team. Quick to announce.

Catch: integration, and still no food depth. Customers do not move themselves.

What this means for AXION

The window is open while incumbents choose between option 1 and option 2. Expect them to move, and to buy. Our lead is food depth in a live product, plus an AI Native engine in AXIS. It lasts only if we keep building AI Native, and do not drift into wrapping.

5 · Built for AI first

AI Native means seven design rules. All of them start from one idea: AI is a first-class user.

A first-class user has its own identity, its own way in, limits set for it, and a record of what it did. Designing for that from the start changes every layer. Adding it later cannot.

The wrong order

Build for people, then wrap it for AI

  • Design screens and interfaces for people.
  • Wrap them as tools through a connector such as MCP.
  • AI inherits what people got: steps sized for a person, no evidence, no undo, limits built for people.
vs
The right order

Design the action once, for AI and people

  • Define each business action with its inputs, limits, evidence and undo.
  • People and agents both use it, under the same rules.
  • Screens, conversation and agent consoles are views over the same actions.

Two reference architectures

The first is the shape most AI Enabled products share. The second is the shape AXIS is built towards.

Why it caps outTools mirror endpoints, so one outcome takes many calls. Authority is a person's session or a shared service account. There is no event stream, so the agent polls or waits. Corrections have nowhere to go, and nothing replays.
Typical stackWeb UI, REST or SOAP gateway, modular monolith with customisation scripts, relational database, plus an LLM service, an MCP server and a vector index added on the side.
Why it compoundsOne action layer serves people and agents. Policy decides what each may do. Every proposal and change is an event, so audit, undo, evaluation and learning all draw on the same record.
Design stanceContracts before screens. Policy as data. Events as the source of truth. Learning and evaluation are product features. Model access sits behind a gateway, so models can change without touching the product.

7 · Why new stacks win

A new stack, and a business starting on it, can build AI better. Not because the models are better, but because nothing is in the way.

Two groups benefit: software vendors that start on a clean design, and businesses that start on new software. Both avoid the same hidden costs.

10 · How FoodFlow learns

How does FoodFlow learn from its mistakes?

Every correction becomes a lesson the business can see, approve and undo, so the same mistake does not come back next week.

1

It gets one line wrong

Sam, on the order desk, gets an email from Harbour Kitchen asking for 2 x 5 kg barra fillets. FoodFlow picks Barramundi whole 5 kg, with 91% confidence. Harbour Kitchen always means fillets.

2 x 5kg barra fillets for Thursday
Barramundi whole 5 kg
FoodFlow's pick, based on the product name
91%

Every system gets some lines wrong. What matters is who catches it, how fast, and whether it comes back.

2

Someone catches it. Who depends on how much FoodFlow may do alone

Prepares for your OKSam sees the match beside the customer's last order and changes it before anything is saved. Nothing reaches the customer.
Acts within limitsThe match goes ahead and shows in the 5:12 briefing. Jordan spots it before picking starts and undoes it in one click.
Runs the workflowThe line is picked and delivered. The customer raises it, and the fix needs a credit note and a re-delivery. FoodFlow steps back one level for this action until it earns the level again.

The more it may do alone, the faster a mistake has to be caught and undone. That is why every action has limits.

3

The correction becomes a lesson, with a scope

Sam fixes the line, then chooses how widely it applies: just this order, always for Harbour Kitchen (recommended), or every customer in this business. A lesson that applies to everyone is reviewed by an owner before it is used.
